What the stack is

A parallel consumer, not a replacement

The existing pipeline is untouched and still publishes /fused_bbox and /tracked_objects exactly as before — verified bit-identical with this stack running, on 1 484 frames, obstacle-only and under full GPU load. object_fusion subscribes to the same inputs, re-runs neither YOLO nor the projection, and publishes its own topics.

Frames. Everything is computed in lidar_tc. A separate ego_frame_publisher adds one static edge lidar_tc → ego, a pure yaw of ego_yaw_correction_deg = −5.35°, because /tf_static declares lidar_tc → base_link as identity and it is not. Three independent methods agree within 0.19°; a fourth (the vehicle self-calibration project) measures −5.4078° ± 0.020, which we have not adopted because it would move the published ego frame for a 0.1 m gain at 100 m.
01

Ground segmentation

Patchwork++ on the full sweep, flags not deletions

A 2D box around a vehicle also contains the road in front of it, and the road is nearer. Any nearest-point rule adopts it. So every sweep is segmented before projection, and each published point carries a ground flag — flags rather than deletions, because the detector needs to know what it is discarding.

02

Near-field levelling

Two passes; levelled labels inside 25 m only

In curves the body rolls and the road banks, so the road plane tilts in the sensor frame and Patchwork++ — which assumes a roughly level road around the sensor — calls road near the car non-ground. The sweep is therefore levelled by the vehicle's own attitude before segmenting.

The attitude → tilt map is ODOM_TILT_COEF, pooled over 2 427 sweeps of two drives: slopes in lidar_tc per degree of odometry roll and pitch, plus a constant.

slope_x = −0.00107·roll + 0.00884·pitch − 0.00919
slope_y = −0.01451·roll − 0.00048·pitch − 0.01419      (degrees in, slope out)
Cross-validated twice. Each drive's own fit held on the other (residual road tilt 1.41° → 0.59° and 1.19° → 0.61° out of sample). The constant row, −0.0142 in roll, equals −0.813°, against a mount roll of −0.865° measured independently by the vehicle self-calibration project — agreement to 0.05° from unrelated data.

03

Point selection inside a box

Three filters, then a median — and a box that may publish nothing

Every point that projects inside the 2D box is a candidate. Three rules run in order, and what survives gives the position as a component-wise median.

04

The depth-jump gate

Withhold one frame, never two

The jumps that remain are one-frame depth spikes: 82% lie along the line of sight (median 5.8 m along, 0.38 m across) while the 2D box stays still in the image. No per-frame selection rule fixed them — centre crops, 3D clustering by size or centrality, prediction-chosen clusters all left 11–13% big jumps. So the measurement is refused instead of repaired.

05

Class vote

A box is sized by the class the tracker has seen, not the latest label

Extent estimation is off, so an object's size comes from a per-class table. That makes a one-frame relabel a resize. A truck passing at 9 m was labelled train for its last 2 of 41 frames, and train in the table is 200 m × 3.2 m — a 200 m bar across the scene.

06

Position covariance

Anisotropic, in the ray frame, from measured spreads

A camera+LiDAR object is far better known across the ray than along it, and the radar is the mirror image. Every measurement therefore carries a 2D covariance built in its own sensor's natural axes and rotated into the frame — which is what lets radar repair range without dragging the object sideways.

Cross-ray, bracketed rather than guessed. It was an invented 0.5 + 0.010·r. Two independent measurements now bound it: the lateral spread against radar azimuth is 0.18–0.78 m, but at every band that is entirely the ESR's own 0.5° — the object's share is unmeasurable with that ruler. The track's own lateral jitter (a second difference, so immune to slow bias) gives 0.045–0.14 m. The model sits between them at 0.10 + 0.004·r. Tightening it fixed the 60–80 m band in the filter (2.0 → 0.9 m) and the error tail (p90 27.2 → 21.1 m).
07

Radar detector

Deliberately dumb: gate, convert, publish

One Detection3D per surviving ESR track, carrying the native polar fields plus a derived Cartesian pose. All association intelligence lives in the aggregator, because converting to Cartesian here would throw away the good axis.

Radar rate is bag-dependent — 29.9 Hz on the selfcal drives, 20.9 Hz on the adps ones — so nothing downstream assumes half a period is 17 ms.

08

Aggregator

Capture-ordered queue, CV EKF, polar radar updates

Three streams at three rates with three latencies cannot be paired two at a time. Measurements are buffered, sorted by their own capture stamp, and released when now − stamp > measurement_lag; each released measurement predicts the filter forward to its own stamp and then updates.

The clock: two rules that look contradictory and are not

Measurements do not arrive in capture order — the camera path is slower than the radar's, and the node counts hundreds of late releases per drive. Two different backwards jumps need two different answers, and getting one of them wrong froze the filter completely.

Three motion bugs, found by watching RViz (2026-09-16). Filtered tracks sat behind their own detections: the box closed at 6.43 m/s while the object approached at 3.01, sitting 0.79 m nearer than its measurement between camera frames. The cause was not radar, association or process noise — every ablation gave the same answer — but the prediction clock rewinding. A measurement released out of capture order (the camera path is slower than the radar's; late counts hundreds per drive) set _last_t backwards, so the next measurement predicted across an interval already applied and ego motion counted twice. Static objects then drifted forward at ~35% of ego speed. Fixed with _last_t = max(_last_t, t): the offset between track and measurement went to +0.00 m and the spurious speed to 0.00 m/s. A second, offline-only bug had the harness pre-load a whole drive into an 8 s twist buffer, which kept its last 8 s and served a twist from minutes later; ego displacement integrated to 86.3% of v·dt. And the first fix created a third bug: on a looping replay the clock pinned at the highest stamp ever seen, so from the second loop onward nothing was predicted at all — the track's range froze (+0.00 m/s while the object closed at 24.99 m/s), sat 6.79 m from its own measurement, and lagged 310 ms. With the rewind rule above: 1.46 m, closing at −24.22 m/s, lag 70 ms, and dt ≤ 0 on 0 of 595 measurements. All three are regression-tested, and the twist buffer now refuses a stamp far before its range instead of clamping.
The merge rule was swallowing cones (2026-09-16). Tracks vanished that the measurements clearly had. should_merge's range-generous clause exists to reconcile a camera branch sitting 14 m short of its radar branch past 80 m — but a line of traffic cones has the same signature: consecutive cones within a degree of bearing, metres apart in range. Measured as measurements with no track within 3 m: 30.6% orphaned, against a 7.8% floor with merging off; association width (29.1%) and the camera gate (25.4%) were not the cause. The clause now requires the two tracks to come from different branches — two camera-backed tracks are two objects the camera saw separately — and a range of at least 60 m, where the camera's range actually is unreliable. That takes it to 17.2%.
…and the rest of it was the Mahalanobis clause, which never bounded a distance (2026-09-16). That clause measures separation in units of the two tracks’ covariance, and a coasting track’s covariance grows without limit: at 2 m sigma the 99% two-degree-of-freedom value spans 8.6 m, past the next cone in a line. So the clause written to collapse a track onto its own duplicate was reaching across to the neighbour. The fix bounds the reach in metres — max_merge_dist = 2.5 m, between a duplicate (under 2 m apart) and the next cone up the line (5 m and more). Measured live over a full 398 s replay loop: orphaned measurements 15.7% → 10.5%, while two boxes landing on one measurement went 1.3% → 3.0%. Tightening the chi-square instead was measured and rejected — it switches merging off entirely (the node logs merged=0 for a whole replay) and doubles boxes to 5.6%.
09

360° LiDAR clusters

Sustain only: a cluster may keep a track alive, never start one

The camera sees about 33° ahead; the VLP-32C sees all around. ground_projection publishes the full-sweep non-ground cloud, lidar_cluster_detector voxelises it at 0.2 m and joins components with a range-adaptive gap of 0.45 + 0.012·r (ring spacing grows with distance, so a fixed gap splits far objects and merges near ones), rejects the shapes an object cannot have, and the aggregator matches what is left to existing tracks within 1.0 + 0.02·r metres, one cluster to at most one track.

Why it may not birth a track. A cluster carries no class: a bush, a kerb and a parked car are the same shape to it. It is trusted to say something is still here, which refreshes the coast budget and nudges the position — nothing more.

It earns its place where the camera has nothing. Following static objects past the moment their track died, against a mirrored-position control for chance, the rear is the best-covered sector of all (65–70% at 15–60 m against ~1% chance); the side is the roof LiDAR's blind zone, which the 0.5 s coast budget already tolerates. Over a full replay loop six objects the car had passed stayed tracked for ~10 s into the side and rear, where none survived before, and paired block-by-block against a clusters-off arm of the same drive the path removes 1.3–1.7 points of orphaned measurements (two independent pairs, both resolved). Cost: 22 ms of a 100 ms sweep.

Two traps, both paid for once. Charging an existence miss when no cluster appears is wrong — coverage is only 40–70% even on an object that is certainly there, so “no cluster” is not evidence of absence; at −0.2 log-odds a sweep it killed tracks wholesale. And a cluster update must move position only: a cluster sits at the footprint centroid while the camera measures the near face, so the difference between consecutive clusters is an offset, not motion.
10

The camera-referenced radar gate

A radar return that disagrees with the camera by more than 3σ is refused

Inside 80 m, where the camera+LiDAR range is trustworthy, a radar return further than 3·sigma_along from the track's own camera range of the last 0.3 s is not applied. Beyond 80 m the gate does not act, because out there the camera range is the number that is wrong.

The evidence for it: at 40–80 m the radar was pulling vehicle tracks more than 3 m off their camera measurement. Asking the LiDAR what sat on that bearing settled who was right — 83% of those returns had nothing at the radar range and a vehicle at the camera range (multipath ghosts), 12.6% were a different object behind the target, and only 1.7% were the camera being wrong. Live, LiDAR-confirmed bad updates fell 119 → 27 for 0.6% fewer radar updates.

Where it costs. In a dense car park the trade is 88 bad updates blocked against 20 good ones lost (4.4:1, against ~46:1 on the reference drive), because a box there often holds a nearer car as well as the target and the camera range belongs to the occluder.
11

Velocity, and when it is published

velocity_valid means distinguishable from standing still

While the vehicle yaws, an object's bearing sweeps and the set of LiDAR returns inside its 2D box changes, so the measured position moves across the ray and the filter reads motion that is not there. Measured on a drive where every object is static, the between-frame jitter goes from 0.77 m/s driving straight to 2.99 m/s while turning, and it is mostly cross-ray.

The estimator is untouched. The uncertainty it cannot see is added where velocity is published: the covariance carries an extra turn_velocity_k · |ω| · range of sigma, and velocity_valid is a chi-square test of the velocity against zero rather than the old “the filter is running and odometry exists”.

Every arrow in that table is false — the drive is static. What had to survive did: on a drive with two passing vehicles, 96.9% of objects above 5 m/s still publish a velocity. k = 1.0 was chosen by measurement, not taste: k = 2 keeps only 58% of real motion, k = 0.5 leaves 22.5% of the false arrows.

For a consumer. velocity_valid = false means no velocity, not a measured zero. The planner bridge already treats it that way. turn_velocity_k = 0 restores the old always-valid behaviour exactly.

Switched off, and why

Built, measured, not enabled
enable_extent_estimation — a BEV rectangle fit for real length, width and heading. Off by the user's decision after a live comparison; the fit is only identifiable within ~40 m, where 0.23 m ring spacing still puts ~6 rings on a 1.5 m body.
enable_camera_only_fallback — ranging a box with no object return from where its bottom edge meets the road. The geometry is now correct (full extrinsic, local ground height; the old version treated image row cy as the horizon and put a 30 m cone at 200 m). It stays off because the residual is 3.3 m at 25 m and 10.1 m at 67 m: one pixel of box edge is 0.81 m of range at 67 m. Cones show a constant, fixable +0.7° offset; vehicles drift −0.08° → +0.91° with range.
enable_radar_only_birth — creating tracks from radar alone. Blocked until the false-alarm rate is measured with the camera as a label; the often-quoted "81.5% radar-originated" is a ratio of candidate counts, not a measured false-alarm rate.
enable_centroid_correction — pushing the visible-surface position back to the object centre. Off because both the LiDAR and the radar report a surface, and correcting one without the other builds a permanent ~2 m inconsistency that looks converged.

Known limits

What this chain still gets wrong
  • Beyond 80 m the camera+LiDAR range is biased, not noisy, and 12.6% of frames there still spike. The fix is radar-owned range, which only acts in filtered mode.
  • Inside 15 m nothing is validated: those objects sit at 15–26° azimuth, the edge of the camera's field of view, where radar coverage is too sparse to serve as a ruler. Range error there reads −3.95 m and should not be quoted as accuracy.
  • Withheld frames blink. 6.3% of detections publish nothing — the marker disappears for one frame rather than jumping.
  • 2.3–5.7% of measurements have no track on them, and past 80 m the band is 1.4–2.4%. Quote that spread, not a single figure: orphans arrive in bursts while the vehicle turns (17.1% of detections against 4.4% driving straight), so one loop carries a ±3 point interval and two arms are only comparable paired block by block on the same drive. The far-band number that earlier versions of this page reported as 30.5% was a defect in the audit, not in the stack — it matched each measurement to a published frame by recording time rather than by capture stamp, and read tracks out of existence.
  • The published position sits beyond the measurement when radar has touched it — +0.45 m, against 0.03 m for a track the radar never saw. That is the radar disagreeing with the camera (the radar itself sits +1.28 m beyond), and 72.7% of tracks land between the two sensors. The tail is the open part: 13.9% sit beyond the radar return, where the return on that bearing is a different object behind the target. (Re-measured 2026-09-23 on the corrected frame match; the same recording read 0.91 / 0.12 / 0.32 m and 51% / 19% when each measurement was compared against a frame published before its own update landed.)
  • A bag rewind drops every track by design; the node logs it as rewinds=.
  • A genuine reclassification lags up to ~2 s behind, by construction of the class vote.
  • Curve evidence is thin: the levelling model rests on 232 + 15 curve sweeps across two drives. A curve-rich, cone-rich drive would firm it up.
  • The radar's lateral mounting offset is unresolved. Fitting it on turning data gives −0.105 m against a surveyed −0.809, but split by turn direction it flips sign (−0.248 left, +0.040 right): sideslip at the sensor has the same signature, so the compensation stays off.
  • Objects exist only where the front-left camera looks. The VLP-32C is 360°; the 360° clustering path is designed but unbuilt.

Every number here is measured on a named bag and reproducible from scripts/: ground_ab.py (point selection), neighbour_ab.py (box jumping, gates, classes), lean_ab.py (ground misclassification), object_ab.py (filter, NIS, ego yaw), radar_ab.py (radar conventions, lever arm), fusion_isolation_check.sh (does this stack disturb the old one). Each harness imports the production rule rather than a copy, and refuses to print metrics until it reproduces what the shipped node published.
